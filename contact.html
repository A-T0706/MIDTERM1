Reflection

Evaluating the design process, AI use, and final decisions behind the project.

Project Reflection
and Final Evaluation

This project explored how Montefiore Square could be improved by focusing on real public-space problems rather than only visual changes. Through research and site analysis, I identified key issues such as lack of shade, limited seating flexibility, and weak neighborhood identity. The redesign process focused on solving these problems by improving comfort, usability, and the overall experience for people who use the plaza every day. The final proposal reflects a balance between environmental improvements, social use, and community identity.

AI played an important role in the development of this project. I used AI tools to generate visual redesign concepts and explore different design directions quickly. AI helped me test ideas related to planting, seating, and overall atmosphere, and it also helped structure and organize parts of the written content for the website. However, the quality of the results depended heavily on the prompts I gave. More specific prompts that included details about the location, user needs, and design goals produced much stronger and more realistic outcomes than general prompts.

AI also had clear limitations. Some generated designs looked visually appealing but were not realistic for the actual site, or did not fully address the real problems identified in the research. Because of this, I had to evaluate each idea critically and decide which elements were useful and which should be rejected. The final design was not created by AI alone, but by combining AI-generated ideas with my own judgment and understanding of the site.

The most important part of the process was making decisions based on real user needs. I had to consider how commuters, students, families, and senior residents would use the space, and how the design could better support them. This led to focusing on shade, flexible seating, and identity rather than just appearance. Overall, AI was useful as a tool for exploration and visualization, but the final outcome depended on human decision-making and critical thinking.
