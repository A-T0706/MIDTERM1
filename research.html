Research

Analyzing the current conditions of Montefiore Square and identifying real design problems and opportunities.

Site Observations
and Design Analysis

The research phase focuses on how Montefiore Square currently functions and what problems affect its everyday use. The plaza is located at Broadway, West 138th Street, and Hamilton Place in Hamilton Heights and was recently renovated in 2022. While the redesign improved circulation and added new trees and seating, several key issues remain. The trees are still too young to provide shade, seating is limited and fixed, and the space lacks a strong connection to the identity of the surrounding community. These observations show that the plaza works well as a pass-through space but does not fully support people who want to sit, gather, or spend time there.

🌳

Shade and Comfort Gap

The plaza includes newly planted trees, but they are not yet large enough to provide meaningful shade. As a result, the space can feel too hot and exposed during warmer months, making it less comfortable for people to stay.

🎨

Lack of Identity

The current design is clean and functional, but it feels generic and does not reflect the culture or history of Hamilton Heights. The plaza lacks visual elements that make it feel like a unique neighborhood space.

👥

Limited Seating Flexibility

Most seating in the plaza is fixed granite, which limits how people can use the space. There are few options for movable seating, making it harder for groups, students, and seniors to comfortably gather or stay.

🛠️

Opportunity for Improvement

The plaza has strong potential because of its location near transit and local activity. This creates an opportunity to improve shade, seating, and identity so the space better supports commuters, students, families, and community use.
